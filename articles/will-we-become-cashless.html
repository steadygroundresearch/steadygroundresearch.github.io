Will We Really Become Cashless? A Global Perspective on the Future of Cash

SG Research · 3 September 2026 · Payment Systems Innovations

Digital payments are transforming how we pay, but the evidence suggests that cash is proving harder to leave behind than many expected.

Looking across the euro area, the United States, Sweden, India and South Africa, the likely future of cash is declining transactional use rather than outright disappearance. Cash increasingly serves specialised roles: a store of value, an option for people who prefer or depend on it, and a fallback when digital payments are unavailable.

What the evidence shows

  • Euro area: cash accounted for around 50% of physical point-of-sale transactions in 2024 and remained the most frequently used payment method.
  • United States: four out of five consumers used cash in the previous 30 days, and cash made up 14% of consumer payments in 2025.
  • Sweden: only 5% of respondents used cash for their most recent in-store purchase in 2025, yet almost half viewed the decline in cash use negatively.
  • India: digital payments have grown rapidly alongside continued demand for cash, which the Reserve Bank of India has described as a “currency demand paradox”.
  • South Africa: cash remains important in rural areas, informal markets and lower-income communities, and the central bank treats its availability as part of a resilient, inclusive payment system.

Why cash persists

Cash offers choice, accessibility for people who face barriers to digital payments, privacy, and continuity when digital payment systems are disrupted. For that reason the future of payments may not be cashless. It may be hybrid.
