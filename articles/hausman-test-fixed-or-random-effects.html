Beyond the Hausman Test: Choosing the Right Panel Data Model

SG Research · 30 September 2026 · Panel Data Methods

Fixed-effects and random-effects models are two of the most common ways to analyse panel data, which follows the same units, such as firms or people, over time. The Hausman test is widely used to choose between them. But should the outcome of a single specification test decide the model?

The test provides useful evidence, but it is not a substitute for judgement about the research question and the data.

Four questions to ask beyond the test

  1. What variation is important for the study: changes within the same units over time, differences between units, or both?
  2. Are unit-specific effects likely to be related to the explanatory variables?
  3. Are there alternative modelling approaches that may better reflect the research question and assumptions?
  4. Are the results robust to reasonable alternative specifications?

The goal is not to find the model that passes a statistical test. It is to choose an approach whose assumptions, use of the available variation and interpretation suit the research question and the data.
